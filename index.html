01 / How I work

I don't trust a model until it beats a boring one.

Most of the job is checking my own work.

A model can score well and still be learning the wrong thing. So I start with the dullest baseline I can find, and anything fancier has to beat it by enough to matter. If a graph neural net can't outdo gradient boosting on flat features, the graph isn't adding anything, and I'd rather find that out myself than hear it from someone else. Then I try to explain the result to someone who wasn't in the room. If I can't, it isn't finished.

Baselines first

Ablations before claims. I report how much a model beats the simple version by, not just its headline score, because the headline score is usually the least interesting number.

Show the working

If a system flags someone's loan application, a person should be able to see why and argue with it. That's why the fraud pipeline comes with SHAP explanations and guardrails.

Numbers people feel

The result I'm proudest of from South Africa isn't a model score. It's cutting data retrieval time by 40% for the 15+ people who depended on it.

01 / NVIDIA · SME lending

GPU-accelerated fraud detection

Organised fraud in small-business lending hides in plain sight: each fake account looks normal on its own. So I look at the network instead. A GPU pipeline pairs graph models with XGBoost to catch account farms and mule rings, and an agent reads the streaming HKMA-IADS data so nobody has to hand-update rules. 03 / Applied research

Quantum error correction with LLMs

Quantum computers make errors constantly, and a decoder has to guess which qubits went wrong. I had LLMs work alongside numerical optimisers to re-weight an MWPM decoder's matching graph, learning from the cases it got wrong. On real data from Google's Sycamore chip, logical error rates fell by 2 to 5%.

06 / Beyond the code

Life outside the terminal.

Pick one.

Always mid-book

There's usually a paper open in one tab and a novel on my desk. Reading is how I keep learning without a deadline attached to it, and it's the habit I'd protect above all the others.

Non-fiction on weekdays, fiction on weekends.

Headphones on, world off

I can't do focused work in silence. Different problems seem to need different soundtracks, and I take that far more seriously than I probably should.

Every project ends up with its own playlist.

Pick-up games and fast breaks

Nothing clears a stubborn bug like a couple of hours on court. It's competitive, it's social, and it's the best excuse I have to stop staring at a screen and go move.

Happier making the assist than taking the shot.

Quick hands, quicker spin

The unofficial tiebreaker for any debate. Fast, tactical, and endlessly replayable in the ten spare minutes between classes. It rewards reading your opponent more than raw power.

Spin over power, every time.

Salt air is the best reset

Growing up between Mumbai and Hong Kong meant the ocean was never far away. It's still where I go when I need to think about nothing at all, which is usually when the good ideas arrive.

Cape Town's coastline set a high bar.

Five cities, three continents

Mumbai, Hong Kong, Cape Town, Buon Ma Thuot, Auckland. Every one of them rearranged how I think about people, problems, and what counts as "normal", which turns out to be useful engineering training.
